Active Areas of Research

The overarching research focus is the development of novel data-surrogate models for diverse applications. I specifically focus on kernel based methods such as Gaussian Process Regression (GPR), with enhancements to address key challenges in quantum chemistry. In addition, I develop active learning strategies to reduce data redundancy.

Machine Learning Method Development

My work concentrates on addressing the bottleneck of expensive high-fidelity computations by building robust, physically informed kernel-based regressors. Using Gaussian Process Regression (GPR), we formulate workflows that scale efficiently without sacrificing the strict mathematical guarantees required by physical sciences.

Multifidelity Machine Learning (MFML)

Multifidelity methods bridge the gap between inexpensive, low-accuracy approximations and costly, high-accuracy reference computations. Standard delta-learning ($\Delta$-ML) operates on a simple difference formulation: $$f_{\text{high}}(\mathbf{x}) \approx f_{\text{low}}(\mathbf{x}) + \Delta(\mathbf{x})$$ We expand on this framework by implementing hierarchical combinations. In our **Optimized Multifidelity Machine Learning ($o$-MFML)** approach, we solve an optimization problem to combine multiple low-fidelity baselines: $$\hat{f}(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{k=1}^M \beta_k f_k(\mathbf{x}) + \delta(\mathbf{x})$$ where the coefficients $\beta_k$ are optimized to minimize the empirical predictive variance across targeted latent spaces, yielding significant computational savings over single-fidelity GPR.

Active Learning & Uncertainty Quantification

Generating training datasets for quantum systems is highly resource-intensive. Active Learning (AL) bypasses this by query-sampling only the most informative configurations from an unlabeled pool. The query selection relies on Kriging variance calculations: $$\sigma^2(\mathbf{x}^*) = K(\mathbf{x}^*, \mathbf{x}^*) - \mathbf{k}_*^T (\mathbf{K} + \sigma_n^2 \mathbf{I})^{-1} \mathbf{k}_*$$ To accelerate convergence, our **Low Fidelity as Bias (LFaB)** sampling scheme utilizes affordable low-fidelity calculations to guide active learning acquisition functions, avoiding redundant computations and achieving optimal sample efficiency.

Domain Applications

Quantum Chemistry with Machine Learning

We map nuclear coordinate configurations $\mathbf{R}$ directly to electronic properties, bypassing explicit solutions of the Schrödinger equation. These methods facilitate long-time scale molecular dynamics simulations of excited-state configurations and photosynthesis-relevant porphyrin-clay systems, producing speedups up to $850\times$ without losing Coupled Cluster ($\text{CCSD(T)}$) accuracy.

Stochastic Modeling in Economics & Finance

Pricing of default swaps is a key process for modelling credit risks. This is of particular interest to financial instruments such as catastrophe swaps which allow insurance companies to hedge against insurance payouts for natural disaster destruction. In previous work, using Martingale process analysis of credit risk, I provided a comprehensive closed form solution of the floating and fixed legs of the catastrophe swap as $$PV^{CDS}_{float} = \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}_{0}\left[e^{-r\tau^{CDS}}(1-\delta)N\textbf{1}_{\tau^{CDS}\leq t_i}\right]$$ and $$PV^{CDS}_{fixed} = \sum_{i=1}^n e^{-rt_i}E^{\mathbb{Q}}_0\left[s_{CDS}N\Delta t_i\textbf{1}_{\tau^{CDS}>t_i}\right] \\ + \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}_0\left[e^{-r\tau^{CDS}}s_{CDS}N\left(\tau^{CDS}-t_{i-1}\right)\textbf{1}_{t_{i-1}\leq\tau^{CDS}\leq t_i}\right]~.$$ The use of a deep UNET architecture to estimate the recovery rate and estiamted losses for hurricanes is also presented.
